## Leg 1 — first beat: even inside the Gate, beaten outside it

- **Where we went.** After the start Wowla got about 150 m ahead (13:45–13:50). We then gained it
  back and led by 40–80 m from 14:05 to 14:15, while we were both still inside the Gate in ~15 kn.
  From 14:15 to 14:45, outside the Gate, we lost ~290 m and were ~200 m behind at 14:55. We rounded
  3.1 min behind (15:00:33 vs 14:57:29). From 14:05 to 14:30 the two boats were never more than
  ~200 m apart across the course, so **side was not the difference; pace was.**
- **Against ORC: 89% while sailing (84% over the whole leg, tacks and route included), mostly
  speed.** We sailed 41° through the water against ORC's 38.9°, and 6.32 kn against a 6.86 kn target
  (92% of target speed). Wowla: 39° against 37.9°, 6.16 kn against 6.46 (95% of its polar while
  sailing, 91% over the leg).
- **Inside vs outside the Gate: what the crew felt.** Inside (30 min, 15.1 kn): 91% of ORC, 39°
  against 38.5°, 6.36 kn against 6.91. Wowla also 91%. Outside (42 min, 12.9 kn, chop about twice as
  steep as inside by our pitch sensor): 88%, **42° against 39.1°, at 6.21 kn against 6.72.** Wowla
  was at 98% there, at 38° and the *same* 6.21 kn. We sailed 3° wider in the waves and gained no
  speed for it. It matches the series pattern: footing in waves does not buy speed.
- **Bow against bow, Wowla pointed higher, inside as well as outside.** Wowla transmits its heading,
  so the two boats can be compared at the same moments on the same tack, within 1 nm, averaging the
  two tacks (a difference in the wind where each boat was, or in its compass, then cancels). Over
  this beat (64 + 27 reports) Wowla was **4.2° higher by the bow and 3.0° higher on its track
  through the water**, with the same ~4–5° by the bow both inside and outside the Gate. So inside the
  Gate, where both boats scored 91% against our wind, Wowla was still pointing higher; the wind where
  it was made up the difference. Outside it was higher *and* at its target speed.
- **Heel was under control:** starboard (heading ~215°) median 24°, over 28° for 17% of the time.
  Port (~288°) 20°, 2%.
- **Tacks were normal, and there were many of them:** 16 tacks from 13:43:40 to 14:56:44, median
  ~10 s lost each, ~2.6 min in total. Five of them came in six minutes (14:03:49–14:09:58) as the
  wind started to veer. Wowla made ~14 side changes (from AIS, approximate).
- **Wind:** 235–242° until 14:05, then a right shift to 257–268° from 14:15, lighter (11–13 kn)
  from 14:25 to 14:40.
- **Outlier: the approach to the windward mark, ~1–1.5 min.** We tacked onto starboard at 14:56:44,
  504 m from the mark, and did not lay it. Tack to port at ~14:59:05 with the helm hard over (35°);

  From 105 m out (14:59:00) the mark took 93 s; laying it at 6 kn would have taken ~35 s. Over the

## Leg 3 — second beat in 19 kn: overpowered, but so was Wowla

- **Where we went.** Both boats went left to the city front, 150–450 m apart. We closed from ~390 m

  vs 16:37:39). **We sailed this leg 1.0 min faster than Wowla on the water** (49.8 vs 50.8 min), but
  the handicap asks for ~2.4 min on a leg this long.
- **Against ORC: 85% while sailing (77% over the leg), almost all speed.** 39° against 38.1°, but
  6.09 kn against a 7.06 kn target: 1 kn slow. Wowla was just as slow against its polar (5.69 kn
  against 6.61, 87%), pointing above its target angle (36° against 37.7°). Bow against bow (30 + 31
  reports) Wowla was again higher: 3.6° on its track through the water.
- **Where the heavy-air loss shows: heel, not a flogging main.** Starboard (heading ~209°) median
  28°, over 28° for 48% of the time; port (~280°) 26°, 36%. In gusts, heel was over 28° for 62% of
  the time, and VMG did not rise (86% in gusts, 85% steady). By heel: 89% of ORC under 22°, 88% at
  22–25°, 86% at 25–28°, 84% at 28–31°, **82% over 31°**. Speed was the same (5.9–6.3 kn) across
  that range; the extra heel went into a wider track through the water (32° → 44°, that is leeway
  and bearing away). Part of this is wind (18.4 kn in the flattest moments, 20.6 kn in the most
  heeled), so treat the size as a hint. But the flattest moments were the best ones: nothing in the
  data says easing the main was what cost us. The logs cannot see the main itself.
- **Tacks: 9, median 14 s each** (normal for 19 kn), ~2.2 min in total. Wowla made ~6.
- **Outliers, both slow stretches in 20+ kn:**
  - Leeward mark exit: after rounding at 15:50:28, speed was 3.6–5.5 kn until ~15:52:05, with the
    helm swinging ±30° and heel from 7° to 41°. About 1.5 min before we were sailing.
  - Two tacks 88 s apart (16:30:42, 16:32:10), then 2 min on port at 4.7–5.3 kn with heel swinging

    is where Wowla got its last 130 m back. Heel swinging like that fits a main being eased right out
    and sheeted back, or a jib not yet trimmed: an inference, not a measurement.

## What we could have done differently

1. **Pace outside the Gate on beat 1. This is the race.** 42° against a 39° target, in chop, with no
   speed to show for it, while Wowla sailed 38° at our speed. Matching Wowla's 91% of polar for the
   whole beat would have been ~6 min, half of the 11.5. Per the crib sheet: groove first, then point
   up a degree at a time while speed stays above the floor. Do not foot in the waves.
2. **In 19 kn, hold heel at 20–25°.** Over 28° for 48% (starboard) and 36% (port) of beat 2, and 62%
   of gust time. Traveller down past 25°; backstay, cunningham and outhaul on hard. If heel still
   won't stay under ~28°, the sail plan is past its range: that is the moment to reef or change down.
3. **Lay the windward mark.** The two extra tacks and the near-stop at 14:59 cost ~1–1.5 min. When
   in doubt near the layline in chop, a few boat lengths of overstand is cheaper than two more tacks.
4. **Fewer tacks and cleaner exits in 20+ kn.** The tacks themselves were normal length, but the 1.5
   min after the leeward rounding and the 2 min at ~5 kn after 16:32:10 were each worth as much as
   several tacks. Sail on until there is flatter water or a real shift, and get the boat going before
   pointing.
5. **Keep doing: the run and beat 2's pace against Wowla.** On the water we gained 1.0 min on beat 2
   and sailed the run at 98%. Beat 2 was the one leg where our pace against our polar was close to
   Wowla's against its own (85% vs 87% while sailing).

At Wowla's 92% of polar over the whole race we would have been 4th and ahead of Wowla; ~93% was 3rd;
a win needed ~101%. The handicap was neutral: worth 0.1 min to us against Wowla and 0.2 min against Feather.

**What "slow" means in breeze.** In 16+ kn neither boat reaches its certificate upwind: across the
series Wowla made 86% of its own and we made 85%. So beat 2's 85–87% was fleet pace, and its lessons
are heel and the windward mark, not boat speed. Beat 1 is different: in 15 kn, outside the Gate,
Wowla was 3–4° higher than us, bow against bow and track against track, and at its target speed,
so that gap is real. Chop cost us speed rather
than angle across the series (see the Methodology), so footing further in the waves is not the fix.

## Data limits

- Wowla's % uses *our* wind and current, and AIS every ~30 s; its per-leg % is ±2–3 points. Its
  roundings matched ours within 8–59 m, so the leg splits hold. Leg 4 has a single Wowla AIS fix.
- Feather, Jarlen and Final Final have no AIS, so they appear only in the whole-race split (Feather
  102%, Jarlen 96%, Final Final 93% of their polars).
- Wowla's tack counts come from 30 s AIS courses and are approximate.
- Instrument wind is unreliable while the boat turns and was noisy at 14:44–14:46 (true wind reading
  4–17 kn within seconds), so the moments there are not treated as round-ups.
- The heel-vs-VMG figures on beat 2 are mixed with wind strength (the most heeled moments had ~2 kn
  more wind). No sail-trim data: the logs show heel and helm, not what the main or jib was doing.
- Chop is from our own pitch, as we meet the waves: a steepness proxy, not a wave height.
