## Leg 1 — first beat: right side with Wowla, but slower than target

- **Where we went.** Both boats went right, and stayed within ~300 m of each other across the course
  all leg. We led on the water by 100–175 m through the middle of the leg and rounded 48 s ahead
  (13:53:09 vs 13:53:57). **Side of the course was not the difference on this beat.**
- **Against ORC: 89%, a mix of angle and speed.** We sailed 43.0° through the water against ORC's
  38.7°, and 6.50 kn against its 6.89 kn target. So we were both lower *and* slower than the target:
  pointing wider did not buy the speed it should have.
- **Against Wowla: same angle, more speed.** Wowla sailed the same 43° but at 6.73 kn, 0.24 kn
  *above* its own target, for 94% of its polar while sailing (91% over the whole leg, tacks and
  route included; we were 89% on both measures). The gap to Wowla on this beat is boat speed, not
  pointing or tactics.
- **Starboard tack was overpowered.** Starboard (heading ~188°): median heel 27°, over 28° for 40%
  of the time. Port (heading ~260°): 25°, over 28° 19% of the time. On the crib-sheet numbers each
  degree past ~25° costs ~0.03–0.04 kn of VMG in leeway alone.
- **Tacks were fine:** 4 tacks, 1–15 s each (the crib-sheet median in 13–16 kn is ~13 s).
- **Wind:** a left phase (210–217°) from 13:30 to 13:45, back to 231° at the top. Current was
  against us along the wind axis (~0.4 kn), equally for both boats.

## Leg 2 — run: on the numbers; Wowla simply outran its polar

- **100% of ORC.** 163° against ORC's 165°, and 7.60 kn against a 7.45 kn target: this leg was
  sailed as the certificate says it should be.
- **Wowla made 106–109% of its own polar:** 8.2 kn at 156° against a 7.6 kn target. The J/100 is
  simply faster downwind in 15 kn than its certificate says. Nothing in our sailing explains the
  1.7 min: it would take sailing *above* our polar to hold them.
- A cluster of 7 side changes 14:15–14:19 near the leeward mark is mostly sailing by the lee around
  dead downwind plus a gybe or two; together they cost well under half a minute.

## Leg 3 — second beat: the right side paid, and one round-up cost about a minute

- **The gain came from the right.** We went further right than Wowla, up to ~700 m right of it by
  14:55. We gained ~300 m on the water from 14:40 to 14:55 while the wind was still *left*
  (207–215°), so the right had more pressure or better current. Then the big right shift arrived
  (237–248° by 15:00–15:10) and paid the right again. We rounded 3:50 ahead on the water, the only
  leg we won on corrected time (+0.9 min).
- **Pace was no better than beat 1: 88–89% of ORC.** 42.0° against a 38.3° target, and 6.44 kn
  against 6.97: 0.5 kn under target in 16 kn with gusts to 19–22. Wowla was no faster here (91%
  median: 44° at 6.51 kn), so on this beat we matched them on pace and beat them on position.
- **Starboard tack was badly over-heeled:** median 28°, over 28° for 52% of the time (port: 25°,
  23%).
- **Outlier: a round-up at ~15:06.** In an 18–22 kn gust, heel reached 36° with the helm at about
  −24° against the weather helm. The boat still turned up (heading 196° → 233°), went head to wind
  (apparent wind ~3 kn), fell off to 150° and was not back to ~7 kn until ~15:08. The tack analysis
  counts two turns there costing ~61 s and ~65 s. **About a minute lost in one gust.** It is the
  "gust becomes heel, not speed" pattern, at its worst. (Worth confirming with the crew: a deliberate
  luff, to avoid a boat say, would look similar in the data.)
- Otherwise 5 tacks at 13–21 s, normal for the wind.

## Leg 4 — reach to the finish: mostly the rating

- A 141° reach in 19.5 kn: we made 8.9 kn, 96% of ORC for the leg. Wowla did 12.0 kn at 141° on its
  asymmetric spinnaker, 105–108% of its polar. **2.3 of the 3.4 minutes is the rating:** the J/100
  certificate expects that reach to be much faster than the Swan's with a symmetric spinnaker. The
  remaining 1.1 min is Wowla out-sailing its polar again.
- A shorter reach, a running finish or a beat finish would have cost us less. Not something the crew
  controls.

## What we could have done differently

1. **Upwind speed, both beats. This is the race.** At 14–16 kn we were ~4° wide *and* 0.4–0.5 kn
   under target. Matching Wowla's pace on beat 1 alone (94% of polar while sailing, tacks
   excluded, against our 89%) would have been ~2.8 min, more than the whole 1.6 min sailing deficit
   on that leg. Per the crib sheet: get to groove speed, then point up a degree
   at a time while speed stays above the floor (6.1–6.2 kn at 14–16 kn).
2. **Keep starboard under ~25° of heel.** Over 28° for 40% (beat 1) and 52% (beat 2) of starboard
   time. Traveller down in every gust past 25°; backstay, cunningham and outhaul on hard from 14 kn.
   (Why starboard more than port is open: sea state on that heading, or trim differing by side.)
3. **No round-ups.** The 15:06 round-up alone was ~1 min, more than the whole beat-2 corrected gain.
   Ease and traveller down *before* the gust hits. In 18+ kn gusts, if heel can't be held under ~28°,
   it is time to reef or change down (with 8 crew instead of 14 this comes 1–2 kn earlier).
4. **Keep doing: the second-beat side call.** Going right paid twice, first in pressure and then in
   the right shift. The run was on the numbers too.
5. **Accept the rating on reach finishes.** A 141° reach finish in breeze costs the Swan ~2 min
   against the J/100s whatever we do; the places to make it back are the beats.

## Data limits

- Wowla's % uses *our* wind and current, and AIS every ~30 s; its per-leg % is ±2–3 points.
- The "round-up" is inferred from heading, heel, helm and apparent wind; instrument wind is
  unreliable while the boat turns, so treat the sequence, not the exact numbers.
- Feather, Tangaroa, Jarlen and Final Final have no AIS, so they appear only in the whole-race split
  (Feather 101% and Tangaroa 100% of their polars).
- No sail-trim data: the logs show *that* starboard was over-heeled, not what the trim was.
